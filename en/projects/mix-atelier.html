Mix Atelier — AI-assisted DJ mashups

Local app that analyses two electronic tracks, prepares transitions and mashups, lets you listen to them, then exports an audio file — with vocal / instrumental separation by an AI model.

TypePersonal project
RoleSolo
PeriodOctober 2026 — ongoing
StatusWorking prototype (V1)

Context

Prepare DJ transitions and mashups between two tracks without paid software or an online service, and understand what makes a transition sound natural. The project is both a complete local web app and a testbed for audio analysis.

Objective

From two tracks, propose coherent transitions and arrangements — good passages, good alignment, no double bass or clashing vocals —, let you listen to them, then export the result as a WAV.

How it works

  • Analysis of each track: tempo and beat grid, key, energy, silences and sections.
  • Alignment to the target tempo by time-stretching.
  • Choice of passages and transitions through explicit, traceable rules.
  • Rendering and export as 24-bit WAV.
  • The only AI component in the app: the vocal / instrumental separation with Open-Unmix (pre-trained model, optional, run on CPU).
  • A transition-ranking model (pairwise logistic regression) exists in the lab, but is not yet trained on real listening preferences.

Work completed

  • App FastAPI + React : import, analysis, transition editor and multitrack mashup editor.
  • Four rule-based generators: alternation, section-based arrangement, vocal / instrumental, continuous instrumental.
  • Automatic diagnosis of the joins between tracks.
  • An evaluation lab separate from the app: inventory and de-duplication of files by their audio content, a leak-free split of the corpus (tracks reserved for evaluation), a blind listening session with a section-annotation editor in the browser, statistical reports and automated tests.

Results

  • The app works end to end: two tracks → arrangement → listening → WAV export.
  • Technical baseline: no errors on 5 pairs × 2 generation modes (alignment within 5 ms, no clipping).
  • Tempo estimated within 2% error on 11 of 13 clips from a reference dataset (UnmixDB).
  • First blind listening test (1 listener, 30 comparisons): the transitions are not convincing yet, and the sample is too small to draw conclusions.

Difficulties

  • Estimating the first beat of each bar and spotting the musical phrases.
  • Avoiding double bass and clashing vocals at the joins.
  • Windows constraints (path length, some models impossible to install).
  • Evaluating quality without ground truth, hence the use of blind human listening.
  • Small corpus: 25 tracks, 24 version families.

What I learned

  • Audio processing: beat grids, time-stretching, level management.
  • Experimental rigour: splitting by version family, blind tests, a statistical policy fixed before looking at the data, freezing the system before evaluation.
  • Architecture of a complete local application, from backend to interface.

Limitations and next steps

  • Limited to electronic music in 4/4 at a stable tempo, and to two tracks at a time.
  • Structure detected by heuristics; source separation leaves artefacts.
  • The ranking model is not trained yet: it needs more listens and several listeners.
  • Next steps: train on human preferences, handle mashups of three tracks or more, port the pre-trained analyser to Linux.
